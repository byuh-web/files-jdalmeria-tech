The Terminator Series

The Terminator | 1984

This is the original film that started it all and introduced the world to the T-800. Skynet sent this machine back in time from 2029 to kill Sarah Connor, not because of who she was, but because of who her son would become. John Connor was destined to lead the humanresistance, so Skynet tried to erase him before he was even born. A soldier named Kyle Reese was sent back to protect her, and if you have not seen it yet, let's just say he ends up being more important to the Connor family than you might expect. Spoiler alert! He is the father.

Terminator 2: Judgement Day | 1991

This one is considered the best of the franchise. The same T-800 model returns, but this time reprogrammed to protect a young John Connor instead of kill him. Skynet upgrades its approach and sends a newer, more terrifying model, the T-1000, made entirely of liquid metal that can shape-shift into anything it touches. Two Terminators, one mission each, and a kid caught in the middle.

Terminator 3: Rise of the Machines | 2003

Connor is now an adult living completely off the grid, trying to avoid a fate he was told he already prevented. Skynet sends the T-X, another upgrade, this time targeting not just Connor but his future lieutenants. He survives again, the guy is basically unkillable at this point, and not literally a machine, but you have to wonder who is the real machine here? haha The twist here is that Judgment Day does not get stopped. It just gets delayed.

Terminator Salvation | 2009

This was my first Terminator film, and it hit differently because it drops you straight into the post-apocalyptic future that the earlier films kept warning about. John Connor is fighting in the resistance in 2018 when a man named Marcus Wright shows up, human enough to feel real, until it turns out he is not. I remember being genuinely shocked, and that moment is what made me go back and watch the entire series from the beginning.

Terminator Genisys | 2015

This one rewrites the timeline in a way that felt off to me personally. Kyle Reese travels back to 1984 expecting to find a weak Sarah Connor who knows nothing, instead she already knows everything and has her own reprogrammed T-800 she calls Pops. Arnold Schwarzenegger plays him, visibly older this time, which actually makes sense within the story. It is creative, but it also erases a lot of what made the original timeline feel meaningful.

Terminator: Dark Fate | 2019

Dark Fate throws out films three, four, and five entirely and picks up after Terminator 2. A new hybrid human-machine called the Rev-9 is sent to kill Dani Ramos, a young woman who will apparently become important to the future resistance. I will be honest, I am still not entirely sure where John Connor fits into all of this. But the action is solid and it feels closer in tone to the Cameron films than the middle sequels did.
