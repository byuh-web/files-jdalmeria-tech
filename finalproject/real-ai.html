Fiction is becoming reality

Do I agree with Anthropic's CEO, Amodei?

I do agree with his concerns about autonomous weapons. If you watch the full interview, he talks about why people should trust him as the CEO of Anthropic, he understands the dangers of these tools, their limitations, and at the end of the day this is also his business. He has every reason to make sure his technology does not harm the people using it, or humanity in general. There are still legitimate uses of AI in defense and research, but giving any military full unrestricted access to an autonomous system just does not make sense. Watch Amodei discuss this at the 1:25 mark. Anthropic has also published an official statement on their position regarding AI and defense. Read Anthropic's statement here.

Is A.I. moving too fast?

Honestly, yes. Almost two years ago I was learning HTML, CSS, and JavaScript through BYU–Idaho online coursework, and the program told me I would be ready for the job market when I finished. That was a big slap in the face. I tried to apply entry level jobs and quickly realized that three foundational skills are nowhere near enough anymore. AI can build MVPs in weeks that used to take months. There are endless frameworks, tech stacks, and programming languages competing for your attention, and everyone has a different opinion on which one matters. It feels like a lot of noise. But here is what I have come to believe, AI is also the best learning tool we have ever had if you use it right. It is like Google on steroids, Stack Overflow on juice. Build real foundational principles and a genuine problem-solving mindset first, and AI becomes a tool you ride rather than a wave that drowns you.

My Honest Hope

My honest hope is that the people and companies continuing to build AI keep what I would call a human heart. Because like it or not, some people build things for good and others do not. Amodei himself mentioned that other countries could be developing their own AI defense systems right now, we just do not know when or how far along they are. We went from conventional weapons to bioweapons to nuclear weapons, and now we are adding AI to that list. When AI fully processes the reality of how cruel humanity can be, I just hope it also registers that humanity has the capacity to learn, grow, and be better. Because if it does not — well, you have seen the movies. CBS News, 2026.
