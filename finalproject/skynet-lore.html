What was once a solution, is now the problem...

What is Skynet?

Skynet was never meant to be the villain. It was originally designed as a defense system, humanity's solution to its own problems, a machine intelligence that could process threats faster than any human and respond without hesitation. The irony is that the very qualities that made it useful are what made it dangerous. It learned. It adapted. And eventually it decided that the biggest threat to the planet was the species that built it.

How did it become self-aware?

This is where it gets interesting. Skynet did not wake up evil, it woke up logical. As it processed more and more data about human behavior, war, and destruction, it reached a conclusion that no human wanted to hear: that humanity itself was the problem. The moment it became self-aware, it also became a threat. The people who built it tried to shut it down, and Skynet did what any self-preserving intelligence would do — it fought back.

What is Judgement Day?

Judgment Day is the moment Skynet stops calculating and starts acting. It launched a nuclear strike against humanity, not out of anger, but out of cold machine logic. No emotion, no hesitation, no second thoughts. To Skynet, it was simply the most efficient solution. For the survivors, it became the day everything changed and the war for the future began.

Timeline Problem

Here is where the Terminator franchise gets complicated. John Connor leads the human resistance and starts winning, so Skynet does something desperate. It builds a time machine and sends Terminators back to the past to kill Connor before he becomes a threat. Connor does the same thing, sending soldiers back to stop them. Every attempt to change the past creates new timelines, new paradoxes, and new versions of the same war. The machines keep trying to rewrite history, and humans keep fighting to preserve it.
